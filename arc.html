Four things worth checking

How to be sure

1. The address, character by character

Everything I send comes from ahmedwalid4499@gmail.com. Read it slowly — the usual trick is one swapped or added character that your eye skates over. The display name is trivial to fake; the address is the part that matters.

2. Your mail app’s own warning

When a message really leaves my Gmail account, Google signs it cryptographically, and the receiving server checks that signature. You do not have to understand any of that — you just have to notice that Gmail is not showing you a “this message may not have been sent by…” warning, or a red question mark where my picture should be. That check is the strongest proof there is, and it happens before you read a word.

3. This page

The email points here, and the section above says exactly which campaign is running and what the email contains. If something arrives that does not match what you have just read — a different book, a different subject line, a different attachment — it is not from me. Go by the page, not the email.

4. Ask me

Write to me at ahmedwalid4499@gmail.com from scratch, in a new message rather than a reply, and ask. I would far rather answer that than have you take a risk on my account.

A short, permanent list

What I will never do

I will never ask you for money, for a payment of any kind, or for card or bank details.

I will never ask you for a password, a login, or a verification code — not for my site, not for Amazon, not for Goodreads, not for anything.

I will never send you to a page that asks you to sign in before you can read the book. The book comes as an attachment, and that is all.

I will never ask you to write a review that says something in particular. Honest is the whole point; a bought review is worth less than none.

If an email claiming to be from me does any of those things, it is not from me. You can forward it to me and then delete it, and you have my thanks for telling me.
